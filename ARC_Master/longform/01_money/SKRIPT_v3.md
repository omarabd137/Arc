# ARC Longform #01 – v3: "The $100 Bill That Explains Everything" (Arbeitstitel; Titel final vor Upload)

Konzept: Wir folgen EINEM 100-Dollar-Schein durch das System. Jake und Ryan (gleiche Straße, gleiches Gehalt) zeigen die zwei Seiten.
Open Loops ab Sekunde 1 (drei Fragen, werden in der Mitte wiederholt und im Finale aufgelöst):
 1) Woher kommt Geld wirklich?  2) Wer bekommt neues Geld zuerst?  3) Warum macht derselbe Gehaltsscheck einen reich und einen arm?
Jedes Kapitel endet mit einem Cliffhanger. Mini-Dialoge, Alltags-Analogien, konkrete Zahlen.
Stil: hochwertiger Cartoon mit realistischen Proportionen, Orten und Requisiten. Ruhige Einstellungen, echte Schnitte, KEIN Rein-/Raus-Zoom.
SFX: nur an echten, sichtbaren Aktionen (Zeilen [SFX]), leise unter der Stimme.

## SECRET #1 – THE DEPOSIT
B5 [VISUAL] Jake (25) betritt eine schöne Bankfiliale (Marmor, Schalter, Logo „First National“).
[SFX] Türglocke beim Eintreten
"Let's follow the bill. Jake gets his first paycheck, and he does the responsible thing. He takes it to the bank."

B6 [VISUAL] Am Schalter. TELLER: "All safe with us!" — JAKE: "Great, thanks!" Jake schiebt den Schein über den Tresen.
"And here's the first thing nobody tells you. The moment that bill crosses the counter, it stops being Jake's. Legally, Jake just lent his money to the bank. What he gets back isn't cash. It's a number in an app. A promise."

B7 [VISUAL] Analogie: Garderobe – Jake gibt Mantel ab, bekommt Marke; hinten verleiht der Garderobier den Mantel an einen Fremden.
"Think of it like a coat check. You hand over your coat and get a little ticket. Except this coat check doesn't keep your coat. It lends it out to someone else while you're inside, and charges them for it."

B8 [VISUAL] Hinter dem Schalter: der Schein geht weiter zu einem Fremden, der im Autohaus einen Schlüssel bekommt.
[SFX] Autoschlüssel klimpern (Schlüsselübergabe)
"Before Jake is even home, the bank has lent his money to a stranger buying a car. At eight percent interest. Jake earns almost nothing. The bank keeps the difference."

B9 [VISUAL] Nacht. Jake schläft, auf dem Nachttisch leuchtet das Handy: „Balance $100“. Schnitt: Banktresor – fast leer.
"So how much does the bank actually have to keep in the vault, in case Jake wants his money back? Since March 2020, in America, the legal requirement is… zero."

B10 [VISUAL] Große „0 %“ auf der Tresortür.
[SFX] Tresortür fällt dumpf zu
"Zero percent. If everyone asked for their money on the same day, it simply wouldn't be there. That's exactly what happened in the 1930s, when thousands of banks collapsed. Today, the government insures deposits up to two hundred fifty thousand dollars, so people stay calm. But that's not even the strangest part. The bank didn't even need Jake's bill to lend money. It can create money that never existed."

## SECRET #2 – MONEY FROM NOTHING

RYAN: "I'd need three hundred thousand." — BANKER: "No problem."
"Three years later, Ryan wants a house. He asks for a three-hundred-thousand-dollar mortgage."

[SFX] Tastatur (während er tippt); Klick (Enter)
"So where does that money come from? Not from a vault. Not from other customers' savings. The banker types it in. That's it. A second ago, that money did not exist. Now it does. And when Ryan pays the loan back, it disappears again."

B13 [VISUAL] Dokument „Bank of England – Money creation in the modern economy (2014)“, Zeile wird gelb markiert.
"This isn't a conspiracy theory. The Bank of England published it in 2014. Most of the money in the modern economy is created by ordinary banks… whenever they make a loan."

"But wait. Isn't money backed by gold? It used to be. Until one Sunday evening in August 1971. Trust that others will accept it. Which means there's no natural limit on how many dollars can be created."

B14 [VISUAL] Vergleich: kleiner Stapel Bargeld neben einem riesigen Bildschirm voller Kontozahlen.

## SECRET #3 – THE LINE

B17 [VISUAL] 1730er-Paris: Richard Cantillon am Schreibtisch mit Feder.
"An economist named Richard Cantillon noticed this almost three hundred years ago. Whoever gets new money first spends it at the old prices. Whoever gets it last… pays the new ones."
