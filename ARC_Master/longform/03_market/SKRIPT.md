## 0:00 HOT OPEN
M0 [VISUAL] New York, Thursday morning. Wall Street, the opening bell. Traders smile, screens green.
"[warmly] Thursday morning. 9:30 a.m. New York. The bell rings on Wall Street, and for a few seconds… it's just another day."

M1 [VISUAL] Screens flip red one after another. Counter starts running: −$100 billion… −$1 trillion… −$2.5 trillion.
"[deep voice] [serious] Six and a half hours later, about two and a half trillion dollars had vanished from the American stock market. That's more than the entire economy of Canada. Gone. In a single day. And nobody stole it."

M2 [VISUAL] Nvidia logo-free chip tower cracks; labels: NVIDIA −9%, MICRON −17%, BROADCOM −10%.
"[dramatically] Nvidia, the most valuable company on Earth, lost about four hundred and fifty billion dollars before dinner. The Nasdaq had its worst day in a year and a half. The Dow dropped more than eighteen hundred points."

M3 [VISUAL] A normal American guy (Leo) on his couch, opening his 401k app. The number drops. He freezes.
"[serious] And right now, millions of Americans are opening their retirement accounts, staring at a number that's a lot smaller than last week… and asking one question. Do I sell everything before it gets worse? Or is this the moment rich people buy?"

M3b [VISUAL] World map at night: Tokyo and Seoul light up red the next morning; screens with −6%.
"[serious] And it didn't stop in America. The next morning, the panic flew across the Pacific. Japan's market fell six percent. South Korea's fell six percent. One bad day on Wall Street, and the whole world felt it."

M4 [VISUAL] Title card: BUY OR SELL?
"[deep voice] Let's find out what actually happened. And what history says you should never do."

## WHY IT HAPPENED
M5 [VISUAL] Election night map, votes counted. Then a giant AI data center, power lines, water pipes, a red stamp: "RESTRICTED?"
"[serious] It started with an election. After this week's midterms, Wall Street got scared of one thing: new rules for AI data centers. Those buildings eat enormous amounts of electricity and water. And if politicians slow them down, the entire AI boom slows down with them."

M6 [VISUAL] A tower of money labeled "AI" built on thin sticks labeled "DEBT". One stick snaps.
"[dramatically] And the AI boom was holding up a lot. For two years, almost everything in the stock market went up because of one promise: AI will change everything, so buy every company that builds it. Some investors didn't just buy. They borrowed money to buy more."

M6b [VISUAL] A chip tower grows taller and taller; a sign "$5,000,000,000,000 – FIRST IN HISTORY"; tiny people at the bottom looking up.
"[serious] Just think about how big this got. In October 2025, Nvidia became the first company in history worth five trillion dollars. Five trillion. More than the entire stock market of most countries. When one company gets that big, and so much of everyone's retirement money is tied to it, even a small crack becomes an earthquake."

M7 [VISUAL] Phone screen: "MARGIN CALL – deposit funds or we sell". Then rows of computers selling automatically, a waterfall of red sell orders.
"[deep voice] When prices fall, borrowed money becomes a trap. The broker calls and says: pay up, or we sell your stocks for you. So they sell. Prices fall more. Computer funds that follow trends see the drop… and sell too. Automatically. In milliseconds. That's how a bad day turns into a historic one."

M7b [VISUAL] Friday morning: a few green arrows appear premarket; Leo looks up hopefully; a question mark over the chart.
"[cautiously] By Friday morning, some of the biggest names, including Nvidia, were already bouncing back before the market even opened. So was Thursday the bottom? Or just the first crack? That's exactly what nobody wants to get wrong."

## THE BIG QUESTION
M8 [VISUAL] Split screen: left a big red SELL button, right a big green BUY button. Leo's finger hovers between them.
"[serious] So here's the question everyone is asking. Is this the start of a crash, or a sale? To answer that, we need two true stories. One where buying the dip destroyed people. And one where selling destroyed them."

M8b [VISUAL] Two doors: left "SELL → safe?", right "BUY → cheap?"; behind each door a shadow.
"[serious] Because both choices can be expensive. Sell too early, and you might miss the comeback. Buy too early, and you might be catching a falling knife. Before you touch that button, here's what happened to people who made each choice."

## STORY 1: 2000
M9 [VISUAL] Year 2000 cartoon office, giant Cisco-like tower "most valuable company in the world", crowds cheering, internet everywhere.
"[dramatically] Story one. The year 2000. The internet was the AI of its time. And the hottest company in the world was Cisco. It built the hardware the entire internet ran on, just like Nvidia builds the chips for AI today. For a moment, it was the most valuable company on Earth."

M10 [VISUAL] The tower collapses floor by floor; the chart falls 90%; years tick by on a calendar: 2001… 2010… 2020… 2025.
"[deep voice] Then the bubble burst. Cisco lost about ninety percent of its value. People who bought the dip… kept buying it, all the way down. And here's the brutal part: it took Cisco more than twenty-five years, until December 2025, to get back to its old high. A quarter of a century of waiting."

M10b [VISUAL] A person from 2000 at a desk, saying "it's cheap now, it can't go lower!"; the chart keeps falling under his feet.
"[serious] And the scary part? Back then, it felt exactly like today. Everyone said the internet would change the world. And they were right. The internet did change everything. But that didn't stop the stocks from crashing first. A technology can be real, and its stock price can still be a bubble. Those are two different things."

## STORY 2: 2025
M11 [VISUAL] April 2025: tariff headlines, the S&P chart diving, panicked people selling on their phones.
"[serious] Story two. April 2025. Tariff panic. The S&P 500 fell almost twenty percent in seven weeks. People sold everything to stop the pain."

M12 [VISUAL] The chart turns around and shoots up to a new record; date stamp "June 27, 2025"; the sellers watch from the sidelines.
"[dramatically] Then it turned. Less than three months after the bottom, the market hit a brand new all-time high. The fastest comeback ever recorded after a drop that big. The people who sold in panic locked in their losses… and watched the recovery from the sidelines."

M12b [VISUAL] Two more charts side by side: 2008 (long, deep valley) and 2020 (sharp V).
"[serious] And it's not just those two. In 2008, during the financial crisis, the S&P 500 fell more than fifty percent. It took years to fully recover. In March 2020, when Covid hit, it crashed more than thirty percent in about a month. And just five months later, it was at a new record. Same market. Completely different speed."

M12c [VISUAL] All four charts lined up; a thin golden line connects every recovery; a label "SO FAR, IT ALWAYS CAME BACK – BUT NOT ON YOUR SCHEDULE".
"[deep voice] So far, the American stock market has come back from every single crash in its history. But nobody gets to choose how long the wait is. Sometimes it's weeks. Sometimes it's years. And that's the part that really decides whether you win or lose."

## WHAT HISTORY SAYS
M13 [VISUAL] A calendar of 20 years; 10 golden days glow; most of them sit right next to the darkest red days.
"[serious] So which story is this? Nobody knows. Not Wall Street, not your neighbor, not me. But history says something that surprises most people. J.P. Morgan looked at twenty years of the S&P 500. If you stayed invested the whole time, you made about ten percent a year. If you missed just the ten best days… it dropped to about six."

M14 [VISUAL] The golden days light up right after the red crash days.
"[dramatically] And here's the twist. Seven of those ten best days came within two weeks of the ten worst days. The best days hide right next to the scariest ones. The people who sell in panic usually aren't around when the market jumps back."

M14b [VISUAL] Old cartoon of Warren Buffett with a quote card; crowds running away from a red chart while one man walks toward it.
"[serious] Warren Buffett, one of the most successful investors who ever lived, put it like this: be fearful when others are greedy, and greedy when others are fearful. Right now, a lot of people are fearful. But even Buffett never said: bet everything you have on one scary day."

M14c [VISUAL] Mini hook card: "BUT THERE'S ONE MISTAKE THAT COSTS MORE THAN ANY CRASH".
"[deep voice] But there's one mistake that has cost regular people more money than almost any crash. And it's the one most people are about to make right now."

M15 [VISUAL] Two characters: one shouting SELL ALL, one throwing all his money on BUY. A third calmly adds a small coin every month to a jar labeled "INDEX FUND".
"[serious] That's why the biggest mistake isn't buying or selling. It's panic. Selling everything because you're scared. Or betting everything because you think it's cheap. Many long-term investors do something boring instead: they keep investing small amounts every month, no matter what the headlines say. This isn't financial advice. It's just what the history books show."

M16 [VISUAL] Back to the Cisco tower: a warning sign "ONE COMPANY ≠ THE WHOLE MARKET".
"[deep voice] And Cisco's story has a second lesson. The whole market came back. But one single company, the hottest one everyone loved, took twenty-five years. Betting everything on one stock is a very different game than owning the whole market."

M16b [VISUAL] Leo looking at two timelines: "money I need next year" and "money for 30 years from now".
"[serious] And there's one question that matters more than any headline: when do you actually need this money? If it's for rent next year, a crash is a real danger. If it's for retirement in thirty years, a crash today might just be noise on a very long road. Same crash. Completely different meaning, depending on your timeline."

M16c [VISUAL] Leo sets up a small "EMERGENCY FUND" jar next to his investments, calm.
"[serious] That's also why many people keep a few months of expenses in cash, outside the stock market. Not to get rich. But so they're never forced to sell their stocks at the worst possible moment, like the investors who borrowed money on Thursday."
