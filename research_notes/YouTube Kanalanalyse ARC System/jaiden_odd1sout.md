Research date: 05.11.2026. Data was collected from Germany.

Data access:
- www.youtube.com channel pages redirect to the consent.youtube.com cookie wall. I did not accept the cookie banner.
- Channel and video data came from two other public YouTube sources: the RSS feeds (`/feeds/videos.xml?channel_id=`) and YouTube's public web data API (browse/player, the same JSON the website loads). It was pulled on 05.11.2026, and these numbers are marked **[observed]**.
- Transcripts could not be retrieved:
  - YouTube's transcript call returned HTTP 400, and caption tracks were not exposed.
  - youtubetotranscript.com returned HTTP 403.
  - A further attempt was stopped by the session's safety classifier as bot-check circumvention.
- As a result, words per minute, jokes per minute and verbatim hooks are **not computed**. See the Gaps sections.
- Social Blade returned HTTP 403.
- vidIQ and TubeAlfred were not used, per the instructions.

Channel IDs: Jaiden = UCGwu0nbY2wSkW8N-cghnLpA; TheOdd1sOut = UCo8bcnLyZH8tBIH9V1mLgqQ.

## 1. Channel packaging (subs, videos, views, cadence, banner, avatar, About, trailer, Community, playlists)

### Takeaway
Both channels package themselves almost entirely around one recognizable cartoon self-avatar:
- **Jaiden:** a white blob-face girl with brown hair, plus her bird Ari.
- **James (TheOdd1sOut):** a white "marshmallow" bean with a tuft.

Their About texts are near-empty and personal. Both now upload long-form very rarely: about 4–6 long-form videos in the last 12 months. Shorts, the Community tab and merch posts keep the channels alive between uploads.

## 2. Content strategy: pillars, outliers, audience psychology

### Takeaway
**Jaiden's pillars:**
1. Relatable life-category storytimes ("My Childhood Obsession with Animals", "Injuries & Being Sick", "Parent Stories").
2. Pokémon and gaming challenge videos. These are her biggest: "I Attempted my First Pokemon Nuzlocke" has 100M views.
3. Pet/bird/cat videos featuring her recurring pet characters (Ari the bird, Tostada the cat).
4. Vulnerable identity and health topics ("Being Not Straight", "I found out I have ADHD.", "Anxiety").

**James's pillars:**
1. Job and everyday-life gripes ("Annoying Customers" 118M, the Sooubway series 98M/101M/68M).
2. "My Thoughts on X" opinion essays (Roommates 104M, Bluey 26M, Gambling 14M).
3. Childhood stories.
4. Animation and internet meta topics (Every Form of Animation 12M, Animation Before Computers 14M).

Outliers come from universal, relatable topics.

### Inferences
- [estimated] The outlier formula for both channels is an **universal everyday category + a first-person angle**: jobs, customers, roommates, junk food, dentists, school, pets, hair. It is not a niche topic. The everyday categories that map to ARC are money anxiety, first job, rent, scams, credit cards and tipping.
- [estimated] James's "Scams That Should be Illegal" (75M) and Jaiden's "Can You ACTUALLY Win Money on Gameshows?" (10M) show money and consumer topics already work in this style.
- [estimated] Audience psychology:
  - Relatability ("this happened to me too").
  - Gentle self-deprecation: Jaiden's "Things I Feel Guilty About" (39M), James's "My Horibal Speling" (97M).
  - Nostalgia: "Growing up Without Cable" (90M), "Strange Video Games I Played as a Kid" (43M).
  - Recurring pet and side characters that viewers feel attached to: Ari, Tostada, James's cats. "Our Cats :3" got 99M and "I got a cat." got 43M.
- [estimated] Both channels' recent medians are 3–10x below their top-60 medians. The format's peak was 2016–2020. ARC should not expect old-era numbers from the format alone.

### Gaps
- Per-video like, comment and retention data was not collected.
- The audience age split is unknown. No primary source was found, but the channels are widely perceived as teen-skewing, which is an unverified impression.

## 3. Script and storytelling (hooks, structure, tangents, jokes, callbacks, endings, WPM)

### Takeaway

- conversational, lowercase, self-mocking titles and descriptions (Jaiden: "totada cat my beloved")
- absurd one-line description jokes (James: "Ikea is such a magical place that I once got lost in there for 14 days. Had a blast though. I wish Sweden was real.")

## 4. Visuals (style, shortcuts, change rate, gags)

### Takeaway
Both channels use extremely simple, high-contrast self-avatars with thick black outlines:
- **James:** a white rounded "marshmallow" bean with dot eyes.
- **Jaiden:** a white blob face with no nose, plus large brown hair.

Emotion is carried by eyes, mouth, sweat drops and squash. The animation is limited and pose-to-pose. James's videos are animated by a small credited team. Jaiden's credits list about 25 named team members.

### Cited Findings
**Character design [observed]**
- Wikipedia describes TheOdd1sOut's characters as "marshmallow people" carried over from his webcomic. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheOdd1sOut)
- Contact sheet of 8 thumbnails inspected on 05.11.2026:
  - IDs: sI1f_fqQc8U, Oc91iOxcB3c, plSyrHqUh78, qF1DTK4U1AM, ppH2tdpwUGY, WfAmK8u0Mx8, 2yFCyPX3kT0, nEQHiJVH79o.
  - URL format: `https://i.ytimg.com/vi/<ID>/hqdefault.jpg`.
- What the thumbnails show:
  - Avatars are pure white with thick black outlines.
  - Faces are minimal: Jaiden has no nose and a flat-line or cat-mouth expression; James has dot eyes with a big open red mouth.
  - Pet characters are exaggerated: Tostada the cat has giant yellow ring eyes.

**Team credits [observed]**
- Jaiden's descriptions carry a "✦ The Team ✦" credit list of about 25 names, e.g. AdamX, Brooskee, PinkPapi, TiffanySeng, Zangeiti. — [Jaiden video sI1f_fqQc8U](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sI1f_fqQc8U), [Oc91iOxcB3c](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Oc91iOxcB3c)

### Inferences
- [estimated] Several shortcuts are visible in the thumbnails and widely known about the genre, but were not verified frame by frame here:
  - mouth flaps
  - held poses
  - flat colour backgrounds
  - reused character turnarounds
- [estimated] The attachment drivers are:
  1. one fixed, ultra-simple silhouette readable at thumbnail size
  2. exaggerated eyes
  3. recurring cute side characters
- [estimated] For ARC, this suggests a fixed mascot or narrator stand-in character with a recognizable silhouette. It does not need to be a self-avatar. Each video's topic would get a cute recurring side character, for example a coin or a piggy bank.

### Gaps

## 7. Thumbnails and titles

## 10. What ARC should adopt and avoid

### Takeaway
ARC should adopt five things:
1. One iconic, ultra-simple mascot silhouette with exaggerated eyes.
2. Recurring cute side characters.
3. Universal everyday-life topics with a casual title voice.
4. Thumbnails built on one face, one strong emotion and one object.
5. Self-contained sketch Shorts, as James does.

ARC should avoid:
- relying on a personal-life "I" storytime voice, since ARC is faceless and narrator-led
- copyrighted game music
- zero-SEO packaging
- the expensive months-per-video cadence that collapsed both channels' recent medians

### Cited Findings (evidence base)
- Universal everyday topics dominate the outliers. "Annoying Customers" 118M, "Roommates" 104M and "Scams That Should be Illegal" 75M (James), and "Injuries & Being Sick" 44M (Jaiden). — [YT-O](https://www.youtube.com/@theodd1sout/videos), [YT-J](https://www.youtube.com/@JaidenAnimations/videos)
- Pet and side characters drive attachment and merch: "Our Cats :3" 99M, "I got a cat." 43M, and Tostada as her best-selling plush. — [YT-O](https://www.youtube.com/@theodd1sout/videos), [YT-J Community](https://www.youtube.com/@JaidenAnimations/community)
- Standalone sketch Shorts have a median of about 6M views, against about 0.5M for behind-the-scenes clips. — [YT-O Shorts](https://www.youtube.com/@theodd1sout/shorts), [YT-J Shorts](https://www.youtube.com/@JaidenAnimations/shorts)
- Infrequent uploads and team costs led to declining medians and downsizing. James's recent-10 median is 5.05M against a top-60 median of 58M, and he downsized his team in early 2026. — [YT-O](https://www.youtube.com/@theodd1sout/videos), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheOdd1sOut)

### Inferences
**Adopt [estimated]:**
1. **Mascot rules.** A white or single-colour bean or blob silhouette, readable at 100 px. Dot or ring eyes carry the emotion: sweat drops, a disgust tongue, glee. This is consistent with the ARC visual-style memory ("cartoon but realistic likeness + real locations"): keep the mascot simple and make the props and locations realistic.
2. **One recurring side character per channel**, for example a pet-like money creature, so viewers attach to it and it can become merch later.
3. **Category-list structure.** "Every type of…" and "Things banks don't tell you" titles map onto the "My Teachers" / "Things that Freak Me Out" storytime structure.
4. **Casual, deadpan titles.** Short, period-ended or parenthetical jokes ("YouTube has Changed (me)", "I got a cat.").
5. **A joke first line in descriptions, then real SEO keywords.** James and Jaiden skip the SEO; ARC should not.
6. **Community tab.** Funny image + question posts (James gets 28–59K likes on these), plus "new video tomorrow" teasers.
7. **Shorts.** Original single-gag sketches and recurring Shorts series (like "Google Maps 1–4"), not trailers.
8. **Brand parody hooks** ("Pika-donald's", "Hey there Adobe") reworked as finance-brand situations, kept YPP-safe with no trademark misuse.

**Avoid [estimated]:**
- Personal-memoir vulnerability as the core hook, since ARC has no persona to draw it from.
- Video-game or third-party music: Jaiden credits "nintendo music".
- Upload gaps of 3–5 months between long-form videos.
- Thumbnail clutter: the Nuzlocke thumbnail is an exception and does not translate well.
- Copying Jaiden's or James's character designs 1:1. This is consistent with the "no 1:1 Crayon copies" memory rule.

### Gaps
- Words per minute, jokes per minute, verbatim hooks and SFX timing remain unverified. Transcripts and audio were blocked.
